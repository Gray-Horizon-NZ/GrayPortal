01 — Philosophy & Voice

Gray is not a colour.
It is a name.

Gray Horizon is a principal-led growth architecture firm — not an agency. Strategy, systems, and ambition sit inside one entity, with the founder directly in every client relationship, not handed off after the pitch.

Founded by Max Fawcett out of genuine adversity rather than a business-school plan — the firm carries that founder's stake in the outcome as part of its identity, not a footnote.

Principle 01

Strategy before execution

Architecture precedes channel work. No campaign starts before the structure underneath it is decided.

Principle 02

Principal-led, always

Direct founder involvement in every relationship — no account-manager layer between the client and the decision-maker.

Principle 03

Retained, not transactional

Long-term partnerships with a limited client roster, not one-off gigs run at volume.

Principle 04

Substance over noise

Data-driven outcomes over trend language. If a word doesn't earn its place, it's cut.

Tone

Confident understatement. A plain statement, then an italic payoff — rhythmic contrast, not hype. No generic agency-speak, no filler adjectives.

Audience

NZ business owners, 20–45, non-gender-specific, growth-stage — looking for precision and depth over agency volume, usually already aware of Max.

02 — Colour

Five tones.
No exceptions.

Pulled directly from the live site's own colour tokens — this is what's actually shipping, not a rounded approximation of it.

Black
#1C1C1C
Page ground. The default surface for anything digital.
Dark
#333333
Secondary surface — cards, elevated panels, icon backgrounds.
Mid
#6B6B6B
Muted text, captions, the italic emphasis colour.
Light
#F4F4F4
Primary text on dark, and the default light-mode ground.
White
#FFFFFF
Peak contrast — headlines, primary buttons, the mark.
Strictly grayscale. Never introduce hue — no blue, green, red, orange, or purple, in any document, deck, or template. This is the most common brand violation in circulation today; see the audit note at the end of this guidebook.
Accent — used sparingly

One job only: making a specific thing pop.

Never a base colour, never a large fill. Two contexts, two golds:

Gold — Web
#C9A227
grayhorizon.nz marketing pages — decks, quotes, client-facing one-pagers. Bright variant #D9BC5C, label variant #B7A15A.
Gold — Portal
#B8A369
grayportal and internal/ops documents — reports, dashboards. Dim variant #3A3626 for backgrounds behind gold text.
Context decides which gold. Client-facing, web-style documents use Web gold. Internal and portal-adjacent documents use Portal gold. Never both in the same document — and never as a substitute for the grayscale palette above, only a highlight on top of it.

Light (300) is the default body weight. Regular (400) for standard UI text. Medium (500) for nav, buttons, and tracked uppercase labels.

Signature device

A plain statement.
Then the payoff, in italic.

Every headline on the site — hero, section intros, the CTA band — follows this exact construction: Cormorant Garamond Semibold for the setup, dropping to italic and muted grey (#6B6B6B) for the closing clause. Reuse this pattern for any document headline; it's the single most recognisable typographic signature the brand has.

Retired from V1.0: Sofia Pro & Open Sans — no longer used anywhere, print included. DM Sans and Cormorant Garamond are the only two typefaces in the system now.
04 — Logo

One mark.
Every context.

The icon (left) is the primary mark shown on documents. The text-only wordmark is its supporting lockup — used wherever the icon needs a name attached, or standalone as a watermark.

  • Icon leads on documents — Secondary.icon2026 is the primary mark. Pair it with the text-only wordmark (Teritary2026) as support, not the reverse.
  • Icon + wordmark together in most cases. The icon alone is rare and needs C-suite sign-off before use.
  • Legal documents use the primary full lockup (icon + wordmark). Watermarks use the text-only wordmark alone.
  • Contrast over consistency — the mark has inverted colourways for light vs. dark grounds. Always pick the version with real contrast against the actual background, never force one onto a clashing surface.
  • Source files: Branding/Logo.Primary.Secondary/ — Secondary.icon2026.jpg (icon, primary) and Teritary2026.png (text-only wordmark, support), each in both colourways.
05 — Layout & Motifs

Restraint,
rendered precisely.

Whitespace
Generous, section-based modular layout. Never crowd a page to fit more in — cut content before cutting space.
Numbered sections
01 / 02 / 03 markers, used only when the content is genuinely sequential — a real process, a walkthrough, a document structure like this one. Not decoration for its own sake.
Dividers
A single hairline rule between sections. Never a heavy box or double border.
Corners
6px radius on rectangular elements, full round on circular ones (avatars, icon badges). Soft, not bubbly.
Imagery
No stock photography. SVG line art and the sun/horizon motif only — see Branding/Supporting.Graphics and Branding/website/Icons for the approved set.
Labels
Uppercase eyebrows in DM Sans Medium, 11–12px, letter-spacing 0.12–0.22em.
Calls to action
Stated plainly — "Request a consultation" — never clever or salesy button copy.
06 — Quick Brand Check

Five questions,
before anything ships.

01

Palette

Grayscale, plus gold only where it's meant to highlight something — no stray colour left over from the source app's default theme (PowerPoint, Google Slides, Word all ship one).

02

Type

DM Sans and Cormorant Garamond only — nothing else, including whatever the document's default template font was.

03

Logo

Correct lockup and colourway for the context and the background it sits on.

04

Voice

Confident understatement, no generic agency filler — a statement, then an earned payoff.

05

Layout

Whitespace, numbering used only where it means something, 6px/full radius, no stock photography.

Known violations, as of this edition: both Client Pitch Deck.pptx files still carry PowerPoint's default rainbow theme and Arial. MSA + SOW Hybrid.docx mixes five unrelated fonts in one file. Neither has been corrected yet — they're the reference examples for what this checklist exists to catch.
